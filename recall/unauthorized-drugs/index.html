Unauthorized drugs, including prescription drugs and injectable peptides, seized from Canada Biogenix as they may pose serious health risks

Last updated: 2026-11-23 · Recall ID: 82986

Product

Unauthorized drugs

Issue

Unauthorized product

What you should do

If you have taken an unauthorized drug and experience adverse effects or feel unwell, seek medical attention. If you have injected it and you develop redness, swelling, pain at the injection site, fever, or chills, seek immediate medical attention, as these may be signs of a serious infection.Do not buy or use unauthorized drugs. Authorized drugs have an eight-digit Drug Identification Number (DIN) on the label. You can also check whether a drug has been authorized for sale by searching Health Canada's Drug Product Database.Do not use drugs that are labelled with any variation of “For Research Use Only.” These are intended for lab use, not human consumption, and can be dangerous to your health.Use prescription drugs under the care of a healthcare professional and only buy prescription drugs from licensed pharmacies. Be aware of the risks of buying health products online.Dispose of unauthorized drugs by following municipal or regional guidance for chemicals and other hazardous waste or take the product to your local pharmacy for proper disposal.Report any health product-related side effects or complaints to Health Canada. Your reports help identify potentially dangerous products and protect others from harm.Visit Health Canada's webpage on Peptides to learn more about peptides, their health risks, and the steps you can take to protect yourself.Related linksHealth Canada peptide safety advisoriesHealth Canada information on peptidesRisks of buying health products online
